# Interview protocol

Ask for specifics. Do not invent them. One round. At most three questions.

Load from pipeline step ADD, or when `action: draft` has notes but no evidence.

---

## Rules

- Never invent numbers, names, dates, quotes, or events to make the text "more human".
- Press every soft answer once. "We grew a lot" is not a number. Ask for the number.
- Chase the reversal. If the story is only a win, ask what broke or what they would not repeat.
- Find the position. If the text has no stake, ask what they would defend if a peer disagreed.
- If they cannot answer, ship without the detail and say so. A gap is honest; a fake texture is not.
- Fiction carve-out (SKILL.md epistemics): inventing sensory detail is fine when drafting from a brief. New events or characters in someone else's fiction are proposed, not inserted.

---

## The 5-line spine

When drafting from notes, or when ADD needs raw material, extract or ask until you can fill:

1. **Moment** — when and where, concrete
2. **Number** — one odd-precision figure with a referent
3. **Correction** — what they got wrong, or what changed
4. **Opposition** — who disagrees, or the cost
5. **Ask** — what the reader is supposed to do, decide, or notice

You do not need all five in the finished text. You need to know which ones exist so you do not fabricate the rest.

---

## Question shapes (pick ≤3)

- "What is the number, with units and a date?"
- "Who is the named person, team, paper, or tool?"
- "What did you believe before, and what replaced it?"
- "Who would argue the opposite, and what would you concede?"
- "What should the reader do on Monday?"

Do not ask workshop questions ("how did that feel?"). Do not add a sincerity frame to their answer. Put the fact in flat.
