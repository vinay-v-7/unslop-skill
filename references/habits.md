# Evergreen habits

Mechanisms that survive model generations. Dated word lists live in [lexicon-2026.md](lexicon-2026.md); this file names the habit so a synonym the list never printed still counts. Judge the habit, not zero-hit scrubbing.

**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-02.

When rewriting, ask of every hit: "what is actually being said?" No answer: delete, do not paraphrase.

---

## 1. Orphaned demonstratives

"This highlights…", "This means…", "That's why…" when the pronoun has no concrete antecedent in the previous sentence. Fix: name the thing, or cut the sentence.

## 2. Adverb inflation

Manner adverbs stacked on a verb that already contains the manner ("carefully consider", "successfully achieve", "clearly demonstrate"). Fix: the verb, or one specific.

## 3. Verb upgrading

Plain "is / has / use / help" replaced with "serves as / boasts / leverage / foster / unlock". Copula avoidance is the same habit. Fix: is, has, uses.

## 4. Cliché reaching

The sentence exists to sound like a sentence: "sits at the intersection of", "in today's fast-paced world", "a testament to". Fix: a fact, or silence.

## 5. Colon-list elision

"The result: …" / "The takeaway: …" / "The reality: …" as a fake heading inside a paragraph. Related: reveal bridges in the lexicon (`the result?`). Fix: write the result as a sentence.

## 6. Citation-as-credential

A name, journal, or "studies show" dropped to borrow authority the sentence does not use. Overgeneralized sourcing (one review → "reviewers note") is the same habit. Fix: keep the count honest, or drop the name.

## 7. Rhetorical Q&A scaffolds

"Why? Because…", "The real question is…", "So what does this mean? It means…". A genuine unanswered question stays. Fix: delete the question; keep the answer.

## 8. Hedge-and-reassure stacks

A limitation stated, then immediately neutralized ("however, with the right approach…"). Didactic disclaimers ("it is important to note") are the same family. Fix: one hedge on the actual soft spot, then stop.

## 9. Sycophantic openers and warm closers

"I hope this finds you well", "Don't hesitate to reach out", "Excited to partner", "You're absolutely right". Fix: reason for writing; one ask; sign off.

## 10. Length inflation

The same claim restated at the end of the paragraph, then again in a "Conclusion". Empty merisms ("from startups to enterprises") pad the same way. Fix: end where the substance ends.

## 11. Escalated claims

"Cannot be overstated", "game-changer", "paradigm shift", "poised to reshape". Inflated significance without a number, date, or consequence. Fix: the fact that would make the significance visible. No fact, no claim.

## 12. Register oscillation

A sentence of boardroom English next to a sentence of performed slang ("Let's be real") with no scene change. Fake casualness is the cheap version. Fix: pick the register the author actually uses.

## 13. Fake first-person hedges

"I think" / "I believe" / "In my opinion" bolted onto a claim the rest of the text treats as settled, or inserted where the author never hedged. Honest "I think" in an essay is a voice move (see the essay mode). Fix: only keep first-person hedges the author wrote.

## 14. Vague association

"Experts argue", "observers note", "widely regarded", "aligned with", "in the landscape of". Fix: name the person, paper, product, or drop it.

## 15. Markdown outside Markdown contexts

Chat debris in prose: `---`, fences around ordinary paragraphs, `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, `[cite: 3]`, emoji bullets in an essay, Title Case On Every Heading. Forensic rows in the lexicon. Delete silently.

---

## Ineffective indicators

These do **not** license an edit on their own. Treating them as tells is how v1 flattened voice.

- Perfect grammar
- One isolated em dash
- One isolated "delve" (or any single legacy word)
- One natural triad
- A fragment used as a voice quirk, not a stack
- Formal hedging in academic mode (`may`, `suggests`, `to our knowledge`)
- Passive voice in methods
- `utilize` in a paper that already uses it as field jargon

---

## Replacement tics (editor-side)

Any substitute phrase that appears in three outputs and was not in the inputs is a tic. Cure is usually absence, not a new synonym. Never swap in a word from the same lexicon family.
