The wind understood what she had done. Nia's throat went dry as she climbed the fire escape over Brick Lane, each step underscoring her guilt. She understood, at last, and accepted that the city would never forgive her.

"You bringing the keys or am I kicking it?" said Jozef, two floors down, eating a cold pierogi from a paper bag.

"Hold on. The latch sticks when it's wet," she said. "Don't you dare kick Mrs Khan's door."

Looking ahead, years later she would stand on this same iron and remember. The alley was more than a shortcut, it was a wound. This matters, the night seemed to say. A dependable moon kept watch.
