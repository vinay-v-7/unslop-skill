# Mode: article

Load when: explainer, how-to, blog, newsletter, news; headings, sources. Token: `article`.
Default tier: strict
Default volume: careful
Structural phases: none under ~2,500 words; above that, longform

## 1 What good human writing looks like here

A reader can use it. Headings name the actual sections. Sources are named and countable. Outlook is a fact or absent. The piece can end without a bow.

## 2 Tells that matter most

1. Vague or canned attribution ("experts argue", "observers note") — [habits.md](../references/habits.md) §6, §14
2. Source-gap disclaimers (disclaimer family in the lexicon)
3. Templated "Challenges and Future Outlook" / awards sections
4. Superficial "-ing" analysis ("…, highlighting…")
5. Promotional verbs with no referent ("boasts", "unlock")
6. "In conclusion" restatement
7. Length inflation: same claim in lead, body, and close

## 3 Not tells here (protect)

- Headings, numbered steps, tables the author wrote
- Direct quotes
- Field jargon
- One "furthermore" in a long piece
- Neutral "is / has"

## 4 Structural work

Do not restructure unasked. If the brief is craft and the piece is long, offer longform. Keep heading hierarchy.

## 5 Voice moves

- Required: every number, name, and source from the input, byte-stable where they are citations
- Allowed: one opinionated sentence if the author had one
- Forbidden: invented quotes, fake "industry reports", new CTAs, synonym cycling of named entities

## 6 Questions to ask the author

Who said it, with outlet and date? What is the number? What is the real risk, with owner?

## 7 Length and volume

500–2,500 words typical. Careful: 80–110% length. News: minimal on quoted material.

## 8 Mode final check

Every attribution has a name or is gone. No outlook section that was not in the source. "-ing" clauses do not assign meaning facts cannot carry.
