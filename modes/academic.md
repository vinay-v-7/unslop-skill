# Mode: academic

Load when: abstract, citations, methods/results, literature review. Token: `academic`.
Default tier: forensic + academic-strict
Default volume: minimal
Structural phases: none

## 1 What good human writing looks like here

Claims scaled to evidence. Citations point at specific works. Methods can be passive and dull. Hedging marks a real limit. Jargon is field language, not decoration.

## 2 Tells that matter most

1. Forensic leakage (cutoff language, chatbot artifacts)
2. Overclaiming ("cannot be overstated", causal language from correlational data)
3. "Sits at the intersection of"
4. Adverb inflation ("clearly demonstrates", "strongly suggests" without numbers)
5. Significance tics ("this matters", "why X matters") in a paper
6. Corrective framing as a topic sentence habit
7. Outlook / "future directions" boilerplate with no programme

## 3 Not tells here (protect)

- "Utilize" and other field verbs
- Stacked transitions in a literature review
- Passive voice in methods
- Formal hedging (`may`, `to our knowledge`, `suggests`)
- IMRAD headings, abstracts, keywords
- **Citations, stay byte-identical** (keys, years, dois, quotes)

## 4 Structural work

None. Do not re-order IMRaD. Do not "humanize" an abstract into an essay.

## 5 Voice moves

- Required: every citation and number unchanged
- Allowed: cutting a significance tic in the author's own prose
- Forbidden: new citations, new p-values, new hedges that change the claim, synonym-cycling paper titles

## 6 Questions to ask the author

Which claims are theirs vs cited? Any sentence they consider load-bearing legal/scientific language?

## 7 Length and volume

Any. Minimal: only unambiguous AI constructions and forensic hits. Abstracts: even tighter.

## 8 Mode final check

Citations byte-identical. No new results. Hedging they wrote still sits on the same claims. Forensic clean.
